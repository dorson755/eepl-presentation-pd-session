Professional Development Session

Building Strong Foundations:
Literacy as the Heart of Early Learning

For Preschool Teachers and Early Childhood Educators

Darnell Lightbourne • Elizabeth Estates Public Library • Commonwealth of The Bahamas

Good morning everyone. Protocol having been established, thank them for the invitation and honor this room of educators. Introduce self and role. Set the central theme exactly: literacy as the heart of early learning. Tone cue: warm, respectful, uplifting.

Opening Frame

Literacy is not only reading words.

It is communication, imagination, confidence, and lifelong learning.

Teachers, families, libraries, and technology each have a role in nurturing it from the earliest years.

Deliver this exactly as setup for the full talk. Emphasize that this is not a reading-only conversation. Name all four partners: teachers, families, libraries, technology. Pause after each phrase so visuals and speech stay synchronized.

Slide 2

Every mickle mek a muckle.

Every little bit adds up to something great.

Introduce the Caribbean proverb exactly. Let it breathe. Make eye contact across the room.

Slide 2

Every mickle mek a muckle.

  • One more story read.
  • One more rhyme sung.
  • One more question answered.
  • One more child encouraged to pick up a book.

Small moments in a classroom become lifelong habits in a child.

Ask the room if they ever wonder whether one small effort matters. Reassure directly: it does. Tie each bullet to building blocks of future confidence and learning.

Literacy begins before independent reading: a voice heard, a page turned, a picture pointed to, a rhyme sung.

Push back against the narrow definition of literacy. Use the exact sequence from speech: listens, speaks thoughts, responds to stories, turns pages, points to pictures, sings rhymes, makes marks. Land on: this is whole-child development.

Emphasis Moment

Reading develops the whole child.

Language. Thinking. Imagination. Empathy. Confidence.

Slow cadence. Name each word with a beat and a pause. This is a message peak. Keep body language grounded and intentional.

Slide 4

The Teacher's Responsibility

  • Read with expression.
  • Invite predictions and questions.
  • Use pictures, rhymes, puppets, and retelling.
  • Make reading part of everyday life.

These are not small techniques. They are powerful acts of literacy building.

Speak directly to the teachers in the room: never underestimate your influence. Mirror the speech wording and keep this practical, affirming, and specific.

Slide 5

Expanding a Child's World Through Books

Children first interpret the world through what they know.

Transition to the Harmony family story. Set context: your two granddaughters and youngest child around the table discussing The Bahamas.

Slide 5 Story Beat

Expanding a Child's World Through Books

"Everybody born in The Bahamas."

"What? I didn't know that!"

A child's world starts small. Books lovingly widen it.

Different countries. Different cultures. Different families. One human family.

Tell the dialogue naturally, with light humor and warmth. Then pivot to the insight: children are not ignorant; they are making sense of limited experience. Books expand perspective safely and beautifully.

Slide 6

Partnership Between Home and School

  • Bedtime stories
  • Dinner-table conversations
  • Simple questions about pictures
  • Family storytelling and retelling

The goal is not burden.

The goal is connection.

Stress that readers are formed in school and at home. Keep this non-judgmental: families are busy, so support should be realistic and joyful.

Slide 7

Preparing Children for a Digital World

Digital literacy should complement, not replace books, conversation, play, and relationships.

  • Age-appropriate tools
  • Guided discussion around digital stories
  • Safe, responsible use
  • Same literacy habits, different medium
Clarify this is not anti-technology. Explain intentional use that protects human interaction while preparing children for the world they live in.

Slide 8

The Preschool Library as a Literacy Hub

A library is more than a building with books.

It is where curiosity is welcomed, questions are encouraged, and imagination is celebrated.

Enter this section from the family story reflection. Reinforce equity: every child, regardless of background, should discover something new.
